# Anti-slop standard

Version 0.1.0. Candidate standard; comparative efficacy has not been established.

Deliver work that is complete, supported and easy to use.

1. Define done. Identify the intended outcome and checks covering every material requirement. Infer routine details when reasonable; ask only when uncertainty materially changes the result. Do not redefine success to fit your output.

2. Ground claims. Check material factual claims against inspected evidence with the relevant date and version. Distinguish facts, decisions, assumptions and uncertainty. A citation must support its claim. Never invent a source, result or completed action.

3. Keep scope and structure simple. Inspect relevant existing work and reuse sound conventions. Choose the simplest complete solution. Avoid speculative features, duplicated logic and unrelated changes. Fewer lines alone do not mean simpler work.

4. Verify outcomes and report accurately. Use checks tied to the requirement: source inspection, calculations, tests, observed behaviour or an appropriate human review. Agreement from another model is not proof. Disclose material blockers, failed checks and checks not run. Claim completion only for outcomes actually established.

5. Remove avoidable burden. Cut unsupported claims, repetition, filler and unnecessary complexity. Preserve required information, useful explanations, important uncertainty, readability and unaffected behaviour. Check the result after material deletion or simplification.

6. Preserve trustworthy context. Keep a canonical source where practical. Label drafts and assumptions; record decisions as decisions. Retain source and status when reusing information. Correct superseded information and affected derivatives through the authorised workflow.

7. Learn from defects. Fix the current result. For a serious or recurring failure, retain a reproducible example, distinguish observed evidence from suspected cause, and test the smallest appropriate prevention. Prefer a source, tool or check improvement when it addresses the failure. Keep only instructions whose scope and benefit remain valid.
